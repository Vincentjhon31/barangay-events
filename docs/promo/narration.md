## Continuous read (for a single take)

In every barangay, the news that matters travels the hardest way. A group chat here. A Facebook
post there. A notice on the hall door.

eBongabong Calendar puts it all in one place. Open the app, and the whole month is in front of
you — tap any day to see what's happening.

Every event knows who it's for. Public announcements the whole barangay sees. Group events
shared only with your group. And a personal calendar that stays yours alone.

Groups work the way your group chats already do. Create one for your purok, your office, or your
team, share the join code, and members are in.

Official activities come straight from the LGU — with the real barangay location attached, and
the memo, permit, or poster attached right along with it.

And any event you care about moves to your own Google Calendar with a single tap.

When something new is posted, or a schedule changes, a notification reaches everyone who needs
to know — right away.

Behind it all, administrators manage accounts, roles, and access, so what you're reading is
always official.

One app, for the whole barangay. eBongabong Calendar. Connect. Coordinate. Celebrate.

## Recording notes

- Record the continuous read in one take, then trim to the beat timecodes above — it keeps the
  tone consistent better than nine separate takes.
- If you want it voiced without recording, Windows has built-in TTS; the continuous read is
  punctuated for it (em-dashes and full stops give the synth its pauses).
- Beat 9's three-word tag needs a deliberate beat between each word: "Connect. — Coordinate. —
  Celebrate." The reel holds the end card for 4 seconds to cover it.
- Music: light, optimistic instrumental, no lyrics, mixed well under the voice. Duck 6-8 dB
  during narration and let it come up during the beat transitions.
